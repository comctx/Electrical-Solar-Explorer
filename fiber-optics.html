Common Connectors & Hardware

LC

Small-form-factor connector widely used on modern network transceivers and patch panels.

SC

Push-pull square-bodied connector common in telecom and premises cabling.

ST

Bayonet-style connector found in many older or industrial installations.

MPO/MTP

Multi-fiber connector used for high-density parallel optical links.

Fusion splice

Fiber ends are precisely aligned and fused together, usually producing very low loss.

Mechanical splice

Aligns fiber ends mechanically without permanently fusing the glass.

Patch panel / enclosure

Protects terminations and organizes adapters, pigtails, and splices.

SFP / SFP+ / QSFP

Families of pluggable transceiver modules used for different speeds, wavelengths, and link types.

Media converter

Converts an electrical network interface to an optical interface and back.

Why Fiber Is Different From Copper

No electrical current in the glass

The information is carried by light, so the fiber itself does not provide an electrical conductive path between endpoints.

Immune to electromagnetic interference

Optical signals are not directly disturbed by nearby magnetic fields the way copper signaling can be.

Long distance / high bandwidth

Low attenuation and optical carrier frequencies support very high data rates over long distances.

More sensitive to handling

Dirty connector end faces, tight bends, damaged fiber, poor splices, and incorrect optics can cause large losses or link failure.

Fiber Safety & Handling

Never look into the end of a fiber or optical transceiver. Infrared light may be invisible but can still be hazardous to eyes. Keep fiber scraps controlled because tiny glass shards can penetrate skin, and clean connector end faces with proper fiber-cleaning methods before mating connectors.
