Beginner Start Here

Electrical energy and signals travel through conductors. The insulation, cable construction, enclosure, raceway, and connector all help make that connection safe and reliable for the intended environment.

Wire

A single conductor, insulated or bare, used to carry current or signals.

Single conductor
Cable

One or more conductors, and sometimes shields or fibers, combined under a common jacket or construction.

Grouped conductors
Raceway / Conduit

A protective pathway that routes wires or cables between equipment.

Mechanical protection
Terminal Block

Provides an organized point where conductors can be terminated, joined, distributed, or disconnected.

Organized termination
Connector

Lets two electrical or signal paths be joined and separated without permanently splicing the conductors.

Removable connection
Junction / Pull Box

Provides an enclosure for splices, conductor routing, pulling access, or equipment transitions.

Access / enclosure
Busway / Busduct

Uses enclosed busbars to distribute larger amounts of electrical power along a building or industrial system.

High-current distribution
PCB Trace

A copper path on a printed circuit board that connects electronic components.

Board conductor
Via / Edge Connector

A via connects PCB layers through the board; an edge connector provides contact pads at the board edge.

PCB interconnect
In Other Words

Raceways, Cable Tray & Routing Systems Technical

Conduit

Metallic or nonmetallic tubular raceway used to protect and route conductors. Bend radius, fill, support, grounding/bonding, and environment all matter.

Cable Tray

Open or partially enclosed support system for permitted cables. Common forms include ladder, trough, channel, and wire-mesh tray.

Trunking / Wireway

Enclosed channel used to route and organize multiple conductors, often with removable covers for access.

Surface Raceway

Mounted on a surface to route power, control, or communications wiring where concealed pathways are not practical.

Flexible Raceway

Used where movement, vibration, or installation geometry makes rigid raceway impractical.

Separation

Power, control, analog, network, and fiber routes may need spacing, shielding, barriers, or separate raceways depending on the system and applicable rules.

Terminal Blocks, Lugs, Ferrules & Splices Technical

These are the connection points that turn loose conductors into maintainable wiring systems.

Terminal type, conductor class, strip length, ferrule/lug style, crimp die, and tightening torque must match the equipment and manufacturer instructions. A connection can look tight and still be electrically poor if the termination is incorrect.

Junction Boxes, Pull Boxes & Cable Glands Technical

Junction Box

Encloses splices or branching connections and provides protection and access where required.

Pull Box

Provides access that makes long conductor pulls or changes in direction manageable without using the box primarily as equipment.

Cable Gland

Secures and seals a cable as it enters an enclosure. Depending on design it may also provide strain relief, armor termination, bonding, or environmental sealing.

Enclosure Rating

Boxes and glands must match the environment: indoor, outdoor, washdown, corrosive, hazardous, dusty, or other conditions.

Bend Space

Larger conductors require adequate space so insulation and terminations are not damaged by overly tight bends.

Accessibility

Many wiring methods require junction and pull points to remain accessible for inspection and maintenance.

Busway / Busduct Advanced

Busway distributes large amounts of power using enclosed busbars rather than individual feeder cables for the entire route.

Feeder Busway

Carries bulk power from one point to another, such as between a transformer and switchgear or between major distribution sections.

Plug-In Busway

Provides tap-off points where plug-in units can feed loads along the route.

Busbar Material

Copper or aluminum conductors are commonly used. Joint design, plating, pressure, and thermal expansion are important.

Short-Circuit Rating

The busway and its tap devices must be able to withstand and interrupt or coordinate with the available fault current as designed.

Voltage Drop & Heating

Conductor size, length, loading, ambient temperature, enclosure, and joint resistance affect operating temperature and voltage drop.

Maintenance

Inspection may include joints, plug-in units, signs of overheating, contamination, damage, and torque/condition checks where specified.

Advanced Cable & Signal Concepts Advanced

Shielding

Foil and braid shields reduce electric-field and RF coupling. Shield termination strategy depends on frequency, grounding architecture, and equipment design.

Twisted Pairs

Twisting conductors helps cancel induced noise and is widely used in instrumentation and data systems.

Characteristic Impedance

At high frequencies, cables behave as transmission lines. Coax and controlled-impedance pairs must match the system impedance to reduce reflections.

Ampacity

Allowable current depends on conductor material, insulation rating, installation method, number of current-carrying conductors, ambient temperature, terminations, and applicable rules.

Voltage Rating

Insulation thickness and construction are selected for the intended system voltage and transient environment.

Fire / Environmental Rating

Cable flame, smoke, sunlight, oil, moisture, chemical, temperature, and mechanical ratings must match the installation environment.

PCB Traces, Vias & Edge Connectors Advanced

PCB Trace

Copper geometry on a board. Width, thickness, length, temperature rise, voltage spacing, and signal behavior all affect design.

Via

Plated hole that connects copper between PCB layers. Power vias, signal vias, thermal vias, blind vias, and buried vias serve different purposes.

Edge Connector

Plated pads along a board edge mate with a socket, commonly in plug-in cards and modules.

Ground Plane

Large copper area used for return paths, shielding, reference potential, heat spreading, or power distribution.

Clearance & Creepage

Spacing between conductive features matters for voltage withstand, contamination, insulation systems, and safety requirements.

Signal Integrity

Fast digital and RF signals depend on controlled return paths, impedance, termination, via placement, and layout geometry.

Correct conductor, cable, raceway, connector, box, busway, and termination selection depends on voltage, current, fault level, environment, temperature, mechanical protection, fire requirements, and applicable electrical rules. Follow equipment instructions and qualified-person procedures for real installations.
