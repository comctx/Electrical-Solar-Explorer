Three-Phase Basics

Three sinusoidal phase voltages are separated by 120 electrical degrees. Balanced three-phase systems transfer power smoothly and are widely used for motors and distribution.

0°
Phase B

Delta vs. Wye

ConnectionRelationshipCommon characteristics
Wye (Y)Line voltage = √3 × phase voltage; line current = phase current for a balanced ideal system.Can provide a neutral point; common in distribution.
Delta (Δ)Line voltage = phase voltage; line current = √3 × phase current for a balanced ideal system.No inherent neutral; closed-loop winding connection.

Instrument Transformers

Current Transformer (CT)

Produces a scaled current for metering/protection. A CT secondary should not be left open while primary current flows because hazardous voltage can develop.

Current measurement
Potential / Voltage Transformer (PT/VT)

Produces a scaled voltage for metering and protective relays while providing isolation appropriate to its design.

Voltage measurement

Transformer Types

Isolation transformer

Transfers power magnetically while providing galvanic isolation between windings.

Autotransformer

Uses a shared winding; compact and efficient for voltage conversion but does not provide isolation between input and output.

Control transformer

Supplies control-voltage circuits in industrial equipment.

Distribution transformer

Steps distribution voltage to utilization voltage for buildings and loads.

Dry-type transformer

Uses air/resin insulation rather than liquid-filled cooling/insulation systems.

Pad-mounted transformer

Enclosed outdoor distribution transformer commonly used with underground distribution.

kVA vs. kW

kVA

Apparent power: RMS volts × RMS amps, including phase relationships.

kW

Real power actually converted to useful work/heat, related to kVA through power factor.

Safety

Distribution and transformer equipment can involve lethal voltage, arc-flash energy, stored energy, and high fault current.
