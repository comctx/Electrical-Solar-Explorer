The Complete Project — Hello to Goodbye

This is the order I would want a homeowner to follow. Do not buy the major equipment first and hope the permit/utility process works afterward.

1
Decide what you want solar to do

Lower the electric bill, provide outage backup, support an off-grid property, charge an EV, or a combination. Your goal determines the architecture and budget.

2
Collect 12 months of electricity use

Use utility bills or account history. Record monthly kWh, seasonal peaks, major loads, rate plan, and whether future loads such as an EV, heat pump, workshop, or electric water heater are coming.

3
Survey the site

Check roof age, usable roof planes, structural condition, ground-mount options, shade, orientation, access paths, equipment locations, service panel, meter, and likely cable/raceway routes.

4
Choose grid-tied, hybrid, or off-grid

Select string inverter, microinverter, optimizer, or hybrid architecture. If batteries are involved, decide what loads must operate during an outage and for how long.

5
Make the preliminary size and layout

Estimate array kW and panel count, sketch the roof/ground layout, identify setbacks and service access, and decide where the inverter, battery, disconnects, and other equipment could go.

6
Check the rules before ordering equipment

Contact the local permitting authority and utility. Find out required drawings, structural requirements, electrical rules, rapid-shutdown requirements, approved equipment/listings, interconnection forms, fees, inspections, and export limitations.

7
Build the design package

Prepare the site plan, module layout, one-line diagram, equipment schedule, conductor/raceway information, grounding/bonding method, overcurrent/disconnect information, labels, datasheets, and structural documentation required by the authority having jurisdiction and utility.

8
Submit permits and utility interconnection

Get the required approvals or conditional approvals before committing to equipment that might not be accepted. Correct plan-review comments before installation.

9
Order the approved equipment

Panels, racking, inverter/microinverters, batteries if used, rapid-shutdown equipment where required, combiner equipment, disconnects, overcurrent protection, raceway, conductors, grounding/bonding hardware, labels, and monitoring equipment.

10
Install the mechanical system

Lay out attachment points, mount racking, preserve roof weatherproofing, maintain required clearances, install modules, manage cables, and install ground-mount foundations/structure when applicable. Follow the racking and roof manufacturer's instructions.

11
Install electrical equipment and pathways

Mount inverter, batteries, disconnects, combiners, junction equipment, raceways, and labels. Route conductors with proper support, bend radius, environmental protection, and separation. The actual electrical connections must follow the approved drawings and equipment instructions.

12
Complete inspection and corrections

Do not hide work that must be inspected. Correct any structural, labeling, wiring, grounding/bonding, equipment-location, or documentation issues identified by the inspector.

13
Utility meter / Permission to Operate

For grid-connected systems, complete the utility's final interconnection steps. The utility may change or reconfigure metering. Do not export or operate in parallel with the grid until the utility authorizes it.

14
Commission, monitor, and maintain

Follow the manufacturer's startup sequence, verify monitoring, record baseline production, test approved backup modes, save drawings/settings/serial numbers, and periodically check for alarms, damage, loose hardware, shading changes, water intrusion, or abnormal production.

What a Permit-Ready Package Usually Needs Technical

Site Plan

Property/building outline, array location, service/meter location, equipment locations, and relevant access or setback information.

Roof / Array Layout

Panel count, orientation, attachment/racking system, roof planes, pathways, obstructions, and required clearances.

One-Line Diagram

Shows the electrical relationship from array through conversion/protection equipment to service or loads.

Equipment Schedule

Manufacturer/model, ratings, listing information, quantities, and datasheets for modules, inverter, batteries, racking, and major protection equipment.

Electrical Details

Conductor types/sizes, raceways, overcurrent protection, disconnects, grounding/bonding, rapid shutdown, labels, and interconnection method.

Structural Information

Roof framing, attachment method, loads, engineering letters/calculations, or ground-mount foundation information when required.

Advanced PV Design Checks Advanced

Cold-Weather Voc

PV open-circuit voltage rises as cell temperature falls. Maximum string Voc after the required temperature correction must stay below the inverter/controller maximum DC input rating.

Current & Parallel Strings

Source-circuit and output-circuit currents, overcurrent protection, conductor ampacity, connector ratings, and equipment input-current limits must all be coordinated.

MPPT Window

String operating voltage should remain inside the inverter's useful MPPT range across expected operating temperatures.

Voltage Drop

Long DC or AC runs increase conductor loss and may affect inverter operation or economics. Use the Wire & Voltage-Drop Lab as a learning aid.

DC/AC Ratio

PV array DC nameplate can differ from inverter AC rating. Clipping, local climate, orientation, export limits, economics, and inverter manufacturer guidance affect the chosen ratio.

Rapid Shutdown / Module Electronics

Required functions and equipment depend on the applicable rules, system architecture, and listed product combinations.

Commissioning: Before You Call It Finished Advanced

Mechanical Inspection

Attachments, module clamps, torque where specified, cable support, roof penetrations, equipment clearances, covers, and weather seals.

Electrical Inspection

Conductor identification, polarity where applicable, terminations, grounding/bonding, labels, disconnects, overcurrent protection, raceway, and equipment settings.

Manufacturer Startup

Use the actual inverter, battery, rapid-shutdown, and monitoring startup procedures rather than improvising a sequence.

Monitoring Baseline

Record initial production, string/module status, battery state, firmware/settings, and alarms so future troubleshooting has a reference.

Backup Test

For approved battery systems, test the intended backup mode and confirm that only the designed loads remain energized.

Project Records

Save permit drawings, inspection approvals, utility PTO, datasheets, warranties, serial numbers, photos, passwords, network settings, and maintenance notes.

Maintenance After Startup

Watch Production

Monitoring trends are often the easiest way to notice an inverter fault, failed module-level device, shading change, or communication problem.

Visual Checks

Look for damaged modules, loose cable management, animal damage, corrosion, water intrusion, failed seals, vegetation, or racking movement.

Cleaning

Clean only when site conditions and manufacturer guidance justify it. Many arrays receive enough natural cleaning from rain.

Battery Health

Review state-of-charge behavior, alarms, temperature, firmware, warranty requirements, and capacity changes where a battery system is installed.

Roof Work

Coordinate future roofing, chimney, HVAC, or electrical work so PV wiring, attachments, grounding/bonding, and shutdown equipment are not damaged.

Keep Documentation

Update drawings and labels if equipment is replaced or the system is expanded.

Final Safety & Approval Boundary

Solar is unusual because exposed modules can produce DC whenever illuminated. Turning off the main breaker does not necessarily remove PV or battery voltage. Use the approved shutdown procedure, equipment instructions, permits, inspections, and qualified-person requirements for the actual installation.
For a grid-connected project, the finish line is not “the panels are mounted.” The finish line is: approved installation → passed inspection → utility interconnection complete → Permission to Operate → successful startup and monitoring.
