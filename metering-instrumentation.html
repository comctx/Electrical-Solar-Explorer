Beginner Start Here

Electrical instruments answer different questions: how much voltage is present, how much current is flowing, how much power is being used, and whether the electrical system is healthy.

Voltmeter

Measures electrical potential difference between two points. A voltmeter is normally connected across the points being measured.

Voltage
Ammeter

Measures current. Traditional ammeter measurement places the current-measuring path in series with the circuit.

Current
Wattmeter

Measures real electrical power by using both voltage and current information.

Power
Multimeter

Combines several measurements in one instrument, commonly voltage, current, resistance, continuity, and often additional functions.

Multiple measurements
Clamp Meter

Measures current by sensing the magnetic field around a conductor, allowing many current measurements without opening the circuit.

Current without series insertion
Oscilloscope

Displays voltage as a waveform over time so you can see shape, timing, ripple, noise, pulses, and transients.

Waveform
Energy Meter / Smart Meter

Measures accumulated electrical energy, commonly in kilowatt-hours. Smart meters can also record interval data, demand, and communicate readings.

kWh
Power-Quality Analyzer

Evaluates voltage, current, frequency, power factor, harmonics, sags, swells, interruptions, and other power-quality conditions.

System health
Pilot Light / Indicating Lamp

Provides a visible machine or panel indication such as POWER ON, RUNNING, FAULT, or READY.

Status indication

CTs and PTs / VTs Technical

Instrument transformers let meters and protective relays work with currents and voltages that would otherwise be too large or unsafe to connect directly.

Current Transformer — CT

The primary current creates magnetic flux that produces a smaller secondary current proportional to the primary. Common secondary ratings include 5 A and 1 A systems, depending on equipment and region.

Current scaling
Potential / Voltage Transformer — PT / VT

Steps system voltage down to a standardized lower voltage for metering, relaying, and control while also providing isolation appropriate to its design.

Voltage scaling
CT SAFETY: Never intentionally open-circuit the secondary of an energized current transformer carrying primary current. Dangerous secondary voltage can develop. CT secondary circuits are handled with approved shorting/test procedures and equipment-specific practices.

Transducers, Shunts & Pilot Devices Technical

Current Transducer

Converts measured current into a standard control signal such as 4–20 mA, 0–10 V, or a digital value.

Voltage Transducer

Converts system voltage into an isolated or scaled output that a PLC, meter, or monitoring system can use.

Temperature Transducer

Accepts a sensor such as an RTD or thermocouple and converts temperature into a standardized signal.

Pressure Transducer

Converts pressure into an electrical signal for control, monitoring, or data collection.

Current Shunt

A precision low-resistance element placed in series. Current is inferred from the small voltage drop across the shunt.

Pilot Light / Indicating Lamp

Connects to a control or status circuit and gives the operator a visual indication. Color meanings depend on the machine standard and design.

Energy Meters & Smart Meters Advanced

Energy accumulation

Energy meters integrate power over time. A 1 kW load operating for 2 hours uses 2 kWh under the simplified constant-load example.

Demand

Many meters track average power over a defined demand interval, which can matter for commercial and industrial billing.

Interval data

Smart meters can record usage in time blocks, allowing utilities or facility systems to examine load patterns over the day.

Communications

Modern meters may communicate readings, alarms, configuration, or power-quality information through utility or industrial networks.

Instrument-Transformer Details Advanced

Ratio

The instrument transformer ratio converts the secondary measurement back to the primary system quantity.

Burden

The connected meter, relay, wiring, and terminals load the secondary circuit. Excessive burden can increase ratio and phase error.

Accuracy class

Metering and protection CTs/VTs are selected for different accuracy and performance requirements.

Polarity

Polarity markings matter for directional relays, differential protection, real/reactive power calculations, and multi-CT measurements.

Protection CT behavior

Protection CTs may be designed to maintain useful performance at high fault current where metering CTs can behave differently.

Secondary grounding

Instrument-transformer secondary grounding is system- and equipment-specific. Follow the exact drawing, applicable standards, and manufacturer requirements.

Measurement Safety

Instrument selection must match the system voltage, available fault energy, measurement category, environment, and the manufacturer's ratings. Use correctly rated leads and accessories, inspect equipment before use, and follow qualified-person procedures for hazardous electrical measurements.
Do not use a general oscilloscope ground clip, meter, probe, CT, shunt, or transducer on an energized power system unless its connection method, isolation, voltage/current rating, and safety category are specifically suitable for that measurement.
