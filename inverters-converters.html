Beginner Start Here

The easiest way to understand these devices is to look at what kind of electricity goes in and what kind comes out.

DC → AC = Inverter

Takes direct current from a battery, solar DC bus, or other DC source and creates alternating current for an AC load.

Example: battery → 120 V AC
AC → DC = Rectifier / Power Supply

Takes alternating current and produces direct current. Rectification is usually followed by filtering and often regulation.

Example: wall AC → electronics DC
DC → DC = Converter

Changes one DC voltage level to another. It may step voltage down, step it up, or do either depending on the circuit.

DC–DC Converter Types Technical

Buck Converter

Steps DC voltage down. In an ideal continuous-conduction model, output voltage is approximately duty cycle × input voltage.

Step down
Boost Converter

Steps DC voltage up by storing energy in an inductor and releasing it to the output at a higher voltage.

Can produce an output magnitude above or below the input depending on topology and control method. Some versions invert polarity; others do not.

Advanced Conversion Concepts Advanced

Switching Frequency

Higher frequency can shrink magnetics and filters but increases switching losses, EMI challenges, and layout sensitivity.

Duty Cycle

The fraction of each switching period that a controlled switch is on. Duty cycle strongly affects ideal buck and boost relationships.

Continuous vs. Discontinuous Conduction

Inductor current may remain above zero throughout a cycle or fall to zero. The operating equations and control behavior can change.

Isolation

Galvanic isolation separates input and output conductive paths, often using a high-frequency transformer and isolated feedback/control methods.

Harmonics & THD

Real inverter waveforms contain harmonic content. Modulation, switching frequency, filtering, and load behavior affect total harmonic distortion.

EMI & Layout

Fast voltage and current edges interact with parasitic inductance and capacitance, making loop area, grounding, shielding, filtering, and snubbing important.

Safety

Inverters and converters can contain hazardous AC, high-voltage DC buses, and capacitors that remain charged after power is removed. This page teaches power-conversion concepts, not live-work procedures. Follow manufacturer service instructions, isolate all sources, verify de-energization, discharge stored energy safely, and use properly rated test equipment and protective devices.
