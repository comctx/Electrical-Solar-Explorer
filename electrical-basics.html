These are the core ideas that make the rest of All Things Electrical easier to understand.

Voltage

Voltage is electrical potential difference—the “push” that can drive current through a circuit.

Current

Current is the rate of electric charge flow through a path.

Resistance

Resistance opposes current flow and is measured in ohms.

Power

Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred or converted, measured in watts.

AC

Alternating current changes direction periodically. Utility power systems use AC.

DC

Direct current maintains one polarity direction. Batteries and many electronic circuits use DC.

In Other Words
Voltage is the push.
Current is the flow.
Resistance limits the flow.

Circuit Basics

Circuit

A complete conductive path that allows current to flow from a source, through the load, and back to the source.

Complete path
Series Circuit

Components are connected end-to-end. The same current flows through every series component.

Same current
Parallel Circuit

Components are connected across the same two nodes. Each branch has the same voltage, while total current splits among the branches.

Same voltage
Open Circuit

A broken or disconnected path. With no complete path, normal circuit current does not flow.

Broken path
Short Circuit

An unintended very-low-resistance path that can allow dangerously high current unless protective devices interrupt it.

Low resistance
Ground / Earth

A reference point and/or protective connection to earth or bonded conductive parts, depending on the system. Equipment grounding normally is not intended to carry regular load current.

Reference / fault path
Neutral

A grounded circuit conductor that normally carries return current in many AC systems. Neutral and equipment ground are not interchangeable.

Circuit conductor
Hot / Live / Phase

A conductor energized at voltage relative to neutral or ground. It can present a shock hazard even when a load is switched off elsewhere.

Energized conductor

Hot • Neutral • Ground

These terms describe different conductor roles in common AC wiring systems.

Hot / Live / Phase

Carries voltage relative to neutral/ground and supplies energy to the load. It can remain hazardous even when current is low or zero.

Neutral

A grounded circuit conductor that normally carries return current in many AC systems. It is part of the normal current path.

Equipment Ground / Earth

A protective bonding/fault-current path. It normally should not carry regular load current.

Neutral and equipment ground serve different purposes and are not interchangeable. Bonding and grounding rules depend on the system, equipment, jurisdiction, and point in the distribution system. Never rely on wire color alone; identify conductors correctly and follow applicable electrical code and manufacturer instructions.

DC Positive • Negative • Ground

DC systems use different conventions, and “ground” does not always mean earth.

Positive (+)

The conductor at higher potential relative to the chosen negative/reference point in a typical positive-ground-referenced DC circuit.

Negative (−)

Often used as the return/reference in battery-powered electronics, but not universally.

DC Ground / Common

May mean circuit common, chassis, signal reference, or earth depending on the design. These are not automatically the same node.

Earth Ground

A physical connection to earth used for safety, shielding, lightning protection, reference, or other system-specific purposes.
