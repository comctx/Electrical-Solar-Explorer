The Troubleshooting Method

1. Define the symptom

What should happen, what actually happens, and when did the behavior change?

2. Check the simple things

Power source, settings, connectors, obvious damage, loose parts, blown protection, and operator conditions.

3. Divide the system

Separate input, control, power-conversion, output, and load sections.

4. Measure deliberately

Choose a test that can prove or eliminate a specific cause.

5. Compare to expected

A measurement is useful only when you know what value or state should be present.

6. Confirm the repair

After correcting the root cause, verify normal operation and that no secondary fault remains.

Open Circuit vs. Short Circuit

Open circuit

Current path is broken. Voltage may appear across the open point while load current falls to zero.

Short circuit

Unexpected very-low-resistance path can cause excessive current limited mainly by source and circuit impedance.

Use the Right Tool

Multimeter

Steady voltage, resistance, continuity, diode checks, and many general measurements.

Oscilloscope

Noise, ripple, pulses, communication waveforms, intermittent dips, and timing.

Clamp meter

Load current, current imbalance, and motor inrush where the meter is designed for it.

Insulation tester

Insulation leakage/resistance on appropriately isolated equipment.

Logic probe/analyzer

Digital states and activity.

Thermal inspection

Abnormal heating can reveal overloads, poor connections, bearing problems, or cooling failures.

Safe Troubleshooting

Start with de-energized inspection whenever practical. Resistance and continuity measurements are normally made with power removed. Live electrical measurements can expose the user to shock, arc-flash, stored energy, and unexpected motion; use appropriate training, equipment ratings, manufacturer procedures, and safe-work practices. Never bypass a safety device merely to make equipment run.
