Beginner Start Here

A power supply converts electrical energy into the voltage and current a circuit needs. A rectifier is the part that turns AC into one-direction current.

Rectifier

Uses diodes to allow current in selected directions so AC can be converted into pulsating DC.

AC → DC
Filter Capacitor

Stores energy between rectified peaks and reduces ripple in the DC output.

Smoothing
Regulator

Helps hold the output near a desired voltage as input and load change.

Stable output
Linear Supply

Often uses transformer, rectifier, capacitor, and linear regulator. Simple and low-noise, but can waste power as heat.

Simple / quiet
Switching Supply

Uses high-frequency switching and energy-storage components to convert power efficiently.

Efficient
Ripple

The remaining AC variation riding on a DC output after rectification and filtering.

Residual variation

Protection & Safety Basics

Fuse / Breaker

Protects wiring and equipment against excessive current when correctly selected and installed.

Isolation

Mains-powered supplies may require galvanic isolation. Never assume low voltage is isolated without verifying the design and documentation.

Capacitor Discharge

Large capacitors can retain hazardous charge after power is removed. Bleeder resistors and safe discharge procedures may be required.

Polarity

DC output polarity matters. Reversed polarity can damage loads and create high current.

Current Limit

Many regulated supplies include current limiting, but its behavior and trip point depend on the design.

Grounding

Protective earth, chassis, signal ground, and DC common can serve different roles and must not be assumed interchangeable.

Mains-powered power supplies can contain lethal voltages and stored energy even after unplugging. This page is for learning and calculation, not a step-by-step construction or repair guide for energized mains equipment.
