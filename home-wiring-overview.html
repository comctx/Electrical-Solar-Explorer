Beginner Start Here

  1. The panel is the distribution point for branch circuits.
  2. Each branch circuit leaves a breaker and serves one or more loads allowed by the circuit design and applicable code.
  3. Switches control loads such as lights; receptacles provide connection points for plug-in loads.
  4. Some areas and appliances use dedicated or specially protected circuits.
  5. This page shows the layout and relationships, not a wire-by-wire installation procedure.

How the Pieces Fit Together

Panel & Breakers

The panel distributes power to branch circuits. Each branch breaker protects the conductors associated with that circuit according to the approved design.

Receptacles

General receptacles may be supplied by branch circuits serving permitted areas. Kitchens, bathrooms, laundry, garages, and outdoor locations often have additional requirements.

Switches & Lights

A wall switch controls the lighting load or a control input. The actual cable arrangement can vary, so this diagram shows the relationship rather than conductor-by-conductor connections.

Kitchen

Kitchen countertop and appliance loads commonly involve multiple branch circuits and GFCI/AFCI requirements depending on the location and current code.

Bathroom

Bathroom receptacles require personnel protection and have specific branch-circuit rules. The exact arrangement depends on the installation and applicable code.

Laundry

Laundry equipment can include 120 V and 240 V loads. A dryer, water heater, HVAC equipment, or range may use dedicated circuits according to equipment requirements.

What the Diagram Intentionally Does Not Show

It does not specify conductor size, breaker rating, box fill, cable routing, receptacle spacing, required circuit counts, service size, conductor colors, or exact terminal connections. Those details depend on the installation, equipment, local requirements, and the electrical code in effect.

Safety Boundary

Educational overview — not a step-by-step house-wiring guide. Service equipment, panels, branch circuits, and fixed wiring can expose a person to lethal shock, arc-flash, and fire hazards. Permits, inspections, utility requirements, equipment instructions, and applicable electrical code matter. Never rely on conductor color alone, and do not assume a circuit is de-energized because a switch or breaker was operated.
