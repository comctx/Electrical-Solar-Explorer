Follow the electrical path from the utility service to a receptacle, light, or 240 V load.

Beginner Start Here

  1. Start at the utility/service entrance and move one block at a time toward the load.
  2. The meter measures energy use; it is not the branch-circuit protection device.
  3. The service disconnect/main and panel distribute power through breakers to branch circuits.
  4. Branch circuits carry power to end devices such as receptacles, lights, fixed appliances, and equipment.
  5. Grounding and bonding provide specific safety functions and are not interchangeable with the neutral conductor.

Meter-to-Load Path

What Each Stage Does

1. Utility ServiceBrings electrical service to the property through overhead or underground conductors.
2. MeterMeasures energy usage for billing. Meter sockets and service conductors can remain hazardous even when branch breakers are off.
3. Service DisconnectProvides the service disconnecting means and service overcurrent protection, depending on the installation.
4. Panel / Load CenterDistributes power to branch-circuit breakers. The neutral and equipment-grounding arrangement depends on whether this is service equipment or downstream equipment.
5. Branch BreakerProtects the branch-circuit conductors from overcurrent. GFCI/AFCI functions may also be required depending on circuit and location.
6. Branch WiringCarries power through cable or raceway to the end device. Conductor size, insulation, temperature rating, and installation method matter.
7. Switches / ControlsControl loads such as lighting. The exact conductor arrangement depends on the circuit design; do not rely on color alone.
8. End DeviceReceptacle, luminaire, appliance, motor, or other load uses the delivered electrical energy.

120 V vs 240 V Concept

Typical 120 V branchUses one ungrounded conductor plus a neutral for the load current, with an equipment-grounding path for fault conditions.
Typical 240 V branchUses two ungrounded conductors. Some 240 V loads also require a neutral; others do not. Equipment grounding is still separate from normal load current.

Protection You Will See in Homes

Breaker / FuseProtects conductors and equipment from overcurrent.
GFCIDetects current imbalance that can indicate leakage to ground and trips rapidly for personnel protection.
AFCIDetects certain arcing signatures associated with damaged or unsafe wiring conditions.

Critical Safety Boundary

Do not treat this page as a wiring procedure. Meter sockets, service conductors, service disconnects, and panels can contain lethal energy, and some parts may remain energized even when a main breaker is off. Utility rules, permits, inspections, manufacturer instructions, and electrical code requirements apply. Service and panel work should be handled by qualified people authorized for that work.

Also: never identify conductors by color alone. Verify the circuit, labeling, documentation, and safe de-energized condition using appropriate procedures and test equipment.
