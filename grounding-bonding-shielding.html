Grounding vs. Bonding

Grounding

Connects a system or equipment to earth or a grounding reference for purposes defined by the system design and code.

Bonding

Connects conductive parts together to establish electrical continuity and an effective fault-current path.

Grounding and bonding are related but not identical. A low-impedance fault-current path is primarily created by bonding conductors and equipment—not by relying on soil as a fault path.

Ground Loop Concept

Simplified loop current = voltage difference ÷ loop resistance. Real grounding and shield systems are frequency-dependent and far more complex.

Shielding & EMI/RFI

Electric-field shielding

Conductive shielding can reduce capacitive coupling from nearby electric fields.

Magnetic coupling

Twisted pairs, routing, loop-area reduction, and suitable shielding materials help manage magnetic pickup.

Common-mode noise

Noise appearing similarly on conductors can be reduced through differential signaling, filtering, isolation, and proper grounding practices.

Signal Ground vs. Protective Earth

Electronics may use analog ground, digital ground, chassis, shield, and protective earth for different purposes. Their connection strategy depends on frequency, current, safety, and equipment architecture.

Do not defeat protective-earth connections to cure noise. Safety grounding/bonding requirements take priority; noise problems should be solved using proper system design, isolation, routing, filtering, and manufacturer guidance.
