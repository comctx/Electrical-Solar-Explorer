Ballasts & LED Drivers

Fluorescent / HID Ballast

Limits lamp current and may provide starting or ignition functions. Ballast type must match the lamp and fixture design.

LED Driver

Regulates power to LED strings. Drivers may be constant-current or constant-voltage and may include dimming, thermal protection, and communication features.

Photocell Control Relationship Technical

A common photocell arrangement senses daylight and switches the lighting load when ambient light crosses its setpoint.

Some photocells require a neutral; others use different wiring arrangements. Never assume wire colors or terminal functions without the actual device diagram.

Occupancy Sensor Control Technical

Occupancy sensors may switch the load directly or send a low-voltage/digital command to a controller or lighting module.

PIR

Passive infrared sensing responds to changes in infrared energy caused by moving warm objects.

Ultrasonic

Sends ultrasonic energy into a space and detects changes in reflected sound caused by movement.

Dual Technology

Combines two sensing methods to improve detection and reduce false switching in suitable applications.

Typical control idea: sensor detects occupancy → controller or relay output changes state → luminaire turns on or moves to a programmed light level → timeout returns lighting to the unoccupied state.

0–10 V Dimming Relationship Technical

Many commercial LED drivers and fluorescent ballasts use separate low-voltage control conductors in addition to the normal power conductors.

0–10 V control conventions and Class 2/line-voltage separation rules depend on the equipment and applicable code. Use the driver's exact instructions.

Lighting-Control Modules Advanced

Relay Panel

Uses controlled relays to switch branch lighting circuits from schedules, wall stations, sensors, or a building automation system.

Dimming Module

Provides phase-cut, 0–10 V, PWM, digital, or other dimming outputs depending on the supported luminaires and drivers.

Networked Controller

Communicates with occupancy sensors, daylight sensors, wall stations, and gateways over a wired or wireless lighting network.

Daylight Harvesting

Uses a light sensor to reduce electric-light output when sufficient daylight is available.

Scheduling

Turns lighting on, off, or to preset levels according to time, astronomical clock, occupancy, or building-management commands.

Load Shedding

Reduces lighting demand in response to energy-management or demand-response objectives where the system is designed for it.

Lighting Safety

Lighting circuits can contain hazardous line voltage even when lamps are off. Electronic drivers and ballasts may contain stored energy. Follow manufacturer diagrams, de-energize and verify before servicing, and use equipment and controls rated for the supply, load, environment, and application.
