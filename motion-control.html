Motion-Control Loops Technical

Current / Torque Loop

Fast inner loop controls motor current, which closely relates to torque in many motor types.

Speed Loop

Uses velocity feedback to make actual motor speed follow a speed command.

Position Loop

Compares commanded position with measured position and generates the motion needed to reduce position error.

Feedforward

Adds predicted command terms to reduce following error during acceleration or steady motion.

Trajectory

Defines how position changes over time, including acceleration, deceleration, velocity, and sometimes jerk limits.

Home / Index

Establishes a known machine reference position so absolute motion can be related to the mechanism.

Braking & Regeneration Advanced

DC-Bus Regeneration

During deceleration or overhauling loads, motor energy can flow back into the drive DC bus.

Braking Resistor

A braking transistor can connect a resistor across the DC bus so regenerated energy is converted to heat when bus voltage rises.

Regenerative Drive

An active front end can return energy to the AC supply when the equipment and grid connection are designed for regenerative operation.

Mechanical Brake

Holding or safety brakes may secure a vertical or gravity-loaded axis when the motor is stopped; they are not the same as dynamic motor braking.

Tuning & Precision Advanced

Gain

Higher loop gain can improve stiffness and error correction but too much gain can cause oscillation, noise amplification, or instability.

Inertia Ratio

Load inertia reflected to the motor affects acceleration, tuning, and achievable response.

Following Error

Difference between commanded and actual position. Drives may alarm if the error exceeds configured limits.

Backlash

Mechanical clearance in gears, screws, and couplings can create lost motion even when the motor feedback is accurate.

Resonance

Flexible couplings, belts, frames, and loads can resonate; notch filters and mechanical changes may be used in advanced tuning.

Electronic Gearing / Cam

Controllers can synchronize one axis to another using programmable ratios or motion profiles.

Motion systems can create sudden high-force movement. Disable hazardous energy and control unexpected restart before working on motors, drives, brakes, couplings, or mechanisms.
