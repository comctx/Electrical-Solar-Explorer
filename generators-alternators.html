Beginner Start Here

A motor turns electricity into motion. A generator does the reverse: motion moves a magnetic field relative to conductors and produces electrical voltage.

Electromagnetic Induction

Changing magnetic flux through a conductor induces voltage. Faster change generally produces more induced voltage.

Generator vs. Alternator

TermTypical meaningOutput method
GeneratorBroad term for a machine that converts mechanical energy into electrical energy.May produce AC or DC depending on design.
AlternatorA generator designed to produce alternating current.Usually uses slip rings or a rotating field with stationary output windings.
DC generatorA rotating machine arranged to deliver direct current.Typically uses a commutator and brushes.
In everyday use, “alternator” usually means an AC generator. In automobiles, the alternator makes AC internally and a diode bridge rectifies it to DC for the vehicle electrical system.

Voltage Regulator

Controls field current to keep system voltage within the required operating range as speed and load change.

Rectifier Bridge

High-current diodes convert the alternator's internally generated AC into DC.

Rotor Field

Current in the rotating field winding creates the magnetic field. Slip rings carry field current into the rotor.

Stator Windings

Usually three-phase windings where AC voltage is induced by the rotating magnetic field.

Portable & Standby Generators

Prime mover

The engine, turbine, water wheel, or other mechanical source that spins the generator.

Governor

Controls engine or turbine speed so frequency remains near its target as load changes.

AVR

An automatic voltage regulator adjusts excitation to help maintain output voltage.

Exciter

Supplies field current to the main generator. Some systems are brushless and use a rotating rectifier assembly.

Transfer Switch

Transfers a building load between utility power and generator power while preventing unsafe source interconnection.

Grounding / Bonding

Depends on system design, equipment configuration, and applicable code. Portable and standby arrangements are not always the same.

Important Generator Safety

Never connect a portable generator directly to building wiring through a receptacle or improvised “backfeed” cord. A proper transfer device and code-compliant installation are required to isolate generator power from utility lines. Generator exhaust also contains carbon monoxide, so engine-driven generators must be operated outdoors in accordance with manufacturer instructions and local requirements.
