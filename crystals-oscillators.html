Beginner Start Here

Crystals and oscillators provide the repeating timing signals that keep digital circuits, radios, clocks, and processors running at precise rates.

Quartz Crystal

A piezoelectric resonator that vibrates electrically at a very specific natural frequency. It helps set the frequency but is not, by itself, a complete oscillator.

Frequency reference
Oscillator

A complete circuit that repeatedly generates an electrical waveform without needing a repeating input signal.

Creates the clock
Frequency

How many cycles occur each second. It is measured in hertz: Hz, kHz, MHz, and GHz.

Cycles per second
Period

The time for one complete cycle. Higher frequency means a shorter period.

Time per cycle
PPM

Parts per million expresses frequency accuracy or tolerance. A small PPM error can accumulate into noticeable clock error over time.

Accuracy
Clock Signal

A repeating waveform used to coordinate when digital circuits change state and perform operations.

Digital timing
In other words: the crystal is the precise resonator; the oscillator is the circuit that uses a timing element to produce a repeating signal.

Oscillator Types

RC OscillatorUses resistor-capacitor timing. Simple and inexpensive, but usually less precise than a quartz reference.Simple timing
LC OscillatorUses an inductor-capacitor resonant circuit. Common in tunable radio-frequency circuits.RF resonance
Crystal OscillatorUses quartz resonance for much better frequency stability than typical RC oscillators.Precise clock
Pierce OscillatorA very common crystal oscillator arrangement that uses an inverting amplifier, a quartz crystal in the feedback path, and load capacitors. Many microcontrollers use this basic topology around their crystal pins.Common MCU crystal circuit
Ceramic ResonatorWorks similarly to a crystal but is generally less precise and often cheaper.Moderate precision
MEMS OscillatorUses a microscopic mechanical resonator plus electronics in an integrated timing device.Modern timing
XOA packaged crystal oscillator containing the resonator and oscillator circuit.Complete oscillator
TCXOTemperature-compensated crystal oscillator that corrects much of the crystal's temperature drift.Higher stability
OCXOOven-controlled crystal oscillator keeps the crystal at a controlled temperature for very high stability.Precision reference
VCOVoltage-controlled oscillator changes frequency in response to a control voltage and is often used in PLLs.Tunable

How a Processor Uses a Clock

Clock Source

A crystal, oscillator module, RC oscillator, MEMS oscillator, or other source provides a reference frequency.

PLL

A phase-locked loop can multiply, divide, or condition a reference to create other internal clock frequencies.

Clock Distribution

The processor distributes clocks to logic blocks so operations occur in a coordinated sequence.

Not One Instruction per Tick

Modern processors use pipelines, multiple clock domains, caches, and parallel execution, so clock frequency alone does not determine performance.

Accuracy, Stability & Real-World Effects

Initial Tolerance

The frequency offset when the device is new and measured under specified conditions.

Temperature Drift

Frequency changes as temperature moves away from the crystal's reference temperature.

Aging

Crystal frequency can slowly change over months and years.

Load Capacitance

The capacitance presented by the oscillator circuit shifts the actual operating frequency relative to the crystal's calibration condition.

Drive Level

Too much crystal drive can affect frequency, reliability, and startup behavior.

Jitter & Phase Noise

Short-term timing variation matters in high-speed digital, communications, RF, and precision measurement systems.

For a real design, follow the processor/IC clock requirements and the crystal or oscillator manufacturer's datasheet for frequency, ESR, load capacitance, drive level, startup margin, supply voltage, layout, and temperature range.
