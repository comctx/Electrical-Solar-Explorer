Vacuum Tube vs. Transistor

Vacuum Tube

  • Uses electron flow through a vacuum.
  • Usually needs heater power.
  • Often operates at comparatively high voltages.
  • Larger, hotter, and more fragile than semiconductor devices.
  • Still valued in some audio, RF, and specialized high-power applications.

Transistor

  • Uses semiconductor junctions or field-effect structures.
  • Does not need a heated cathode.
  • Can operate at much lower voltages and power levels.
  • Small enough to integrate by millions or billions on a chip.
  • Dominates modern electronics, computing, control, and power conversion.

Tube Equipment Safety

High Voltage

Plate supplies commonly use voltages high enough to cause serious injury or death.

Stored Charge

Filter capacitors can remain charged after power is removed.

Heat

Tubes, resistors, transformers, and surrounding parts can become hot enough to burn skin.

Glass Envelope

Tubes are fragile glass devices and can crack or break if mishandled or thermally stressed.

Do not probe or service energized tube equipment unless you are properly trained and equipped for high-voltage work. Unplugging equipment does not guarantee that internal capacitors are discharged.
