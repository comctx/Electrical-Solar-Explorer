Beginner Start Here

New to inductors and coils? These six ideas explain the page before you get into the calculators.

What is an inductor?

An inductor is usually a coil of wire. When current flows through the coil, it creates a magnetic field and stores energy in that field.

What does inductance mean?

Inductance tells you how strongly a coil resists a change in current. Its unit is the henry, written H. Smaller values are often shown in mH or µH.

Why does current rise slowly?

When power is first applied, the growing magnetic field creates a voltage that opposes the sudden increase in current. The current therefore builds instead of jumping instantly.

Why does current resist stopping?

When power is removed, the magnetic field collapses. That collapsing field can create a voltage that tries to keep the current flowing. This is called back-EMF or flyback.

What does the core do?

The material inside or around the coil changes how strongly the magnetic field is concentrated. Air, ferrite, iron, and powdered-iron cores are used for different frequencies and power levels.

Where are inductors used?

You find them in power supplies, filters, radios, motors, relays, solenoids, transformers, noise suppressors, and many other electronic and electrical systems.

In Other Words
A resistor opposes current flow.
A capacitor resists changes in voltage.
An inductor resists changes in current.

Coil & Core Types

A quick guide to common inductor and coil constructions.

Air-Core Coil No magnetic core. Useful at high frequencies and where low core loss matters. RF / tuning
Ferrite-Core Inductor Ferrite increases inductance and is common in switching supplies, RF chokes, and EMI filtering. High frequency
Iron / Powdered-Iron Core Useful for energy storage and lower-frequency power applications. Core saturation must be considered. Power inductors
Toroidal Inductor Wire is wound around a ring-shaped core. The magnetic field is well contained, reducing stray field. Compact / low stray field
Choke An inductor used to impede unwanted AC or noise while allowing DC or lower-frequency current to pass. Filtering
Solenoid / Electromagnet A coil that creates mechanical magnetic force when energized, used in valves, relays, locks, and actuators. Motion / force
When current through an inductor is interrupted, the collapsing magnetic field can create a high-voltage back-EMF spike. Real relay, solenoid, and motor circuits often require appropriate suppression components.
