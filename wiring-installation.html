Beginner Start Here

These are the basic terms used when talking about electrical wiring and installation.

Conductor

A material that carries electric current. Copper and aluminum are common wiring conductors.

Carries current
Insulation

A non-conductive covering around a conductor that helps prevent unwanted contact, leakage, and short circuits.

Protective covering
AWG

American Wire Gauge is a standardized way of describing conductor size. In AWG, a smaller gauge number generally means a larger conductor.

Wire size
Ampacity

The maximum current a conductor is permitted to carry under specified conditions without exceeding temperature limits.

Current capability
Ground Fault / Earth Leakage

Unwanted current flowing to ground or earth instead of remaining in the intended circuit path. GFCI/RCD devices detect current imbalance and can disconnect the circuit.

Fault current
Overcurrent

Current above what a conductor, circuit, or equipment is designed to carry. Fuses and circuit breakers are common overcurrent-protection devices.

Too much current
Load

A device or piece of equipment that uses electrical power—for example a lamp, heater, motor, appliance, or electronic circuit.

Consumes power

Copper vs. Aluminum Conductors

Copper

High conductivity, common in branch circuits and electronics, and mechanically compact for a given resistance.

Aluminum

Lighter and often economical for larger conductors, but terminations, conductor size, connector ratings, oxide control, and installation practices matter.

Never assume a connector or device listed for copper is also suitable for aluminum. Use conductors, terminals, compounds, and installation methods approved for the actual material and application.

Installation Basics

Conductor Identification

Color can help, but it should never be the only method used to determine conductor function.

Terminations

Connections must match conductor material, size, temperature rating, and terminal listing, and be tightened according to equipment instructions.

Protection

Breakers and fuses protect conductors and equipment against overcurrent when properly selected and installed.

Environment

Wet locations, heat, sunlight, burial, chemicals, vibration, and physical damage all affect wiring-method and insulation choices.

This page teaches concepts, not jurisdiction-specific installation instructions. Electrical work can present shock, arc-flash, and fire hazards. Follow applicable code, permits, equipment instructions, and qualified-person requirements for real installations.
