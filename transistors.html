Beginner Start Here

Transistors can look complicated, but the basic idea is simple: a small electrical signal controls a larger current or voltage.

What is a transistor?

A transistor is a semiconductor device used mainly as an electronic switch or amplifier.

BJT vs. MOSFET

A BJT is controlled by base current. A MOSFET is controlled mainly by voltage between its gate and source.

Switching

In switching applications, the goal is usually to move between OFF and ON states with as little power loss as practical.

Amplification

In analog circuits, a transistor can make a small changing signal control a larger changing signal.

NPN / PNP and N-channel / P-channel

These complementary types use opposite polarities and circuit arrangements. NPN and N-channel devices are especially common in low-side switching.

Ratings matter

Voltage, current, power, temperature, gain, gate drive, and safe operating area all matter. The exact part datasheet controls real designs.

In Other Words
BJT: a small base current controls a larger collector current.
MOSFET: gate-to-source voltage controls the drain-source path.
Switching transistors work best when clearly OFF or appropriately driven ON.

BJT vs. MOSFET

Both can switch and amplify, but they are driven differently.

BJT

  • Terminals: base, collector, emitter.
  • Base current controls collector current.
  • Switching designs often use enough base current to reach low Vce(sat).
  • Can be very useful in small-signal amplification and simple switching.

MOSFET

  • Terminals: gate, drain, source.
  • Gate voltage controls the drain-source channel.
  • DC gate current is ideally very small, though the gate capacitance must be charged and discharged during switching.
  • Power MOSFETs are common in efficient switching and power conversion.
