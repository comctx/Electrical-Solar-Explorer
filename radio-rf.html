Beginner Start Here

RF

Radio frequency refers to alternating electrical signals and electromagnetic waves used for wireless communication and sensing.

Electromagnetic waves
Antenna

Converts electrical RF energy into electromagnetic waves, or received waves back into electrical signals.

Frequency

How many cycles occur each second, measured in hertz.

Wavelength

Physical distance a wave travels during one cycle.

Modulation

Places information onto a carrier by changing amplitude, frequency, phase, or a digital equivalent.

Impedance

AC opposition to current that includes resistance and reactance; RF systems often use standardized impedances such as 50 Ω or 75 Ω.

Coax & Transmission Lines

50 Ω coax

Common in radio transmitters, antennas, instrumentation, and data systems.

75 Ω coax

Common in television, video, and cable-distribution systems.

Characteristic impedance

A transmission-line property set by geometry and dielectric material, especially important when line length is electrically significant.

Inside a Radio

RF filter

Selects desired frequency ranges and rejects unwanted signals.

Mixer

Combines signals to create sum/difference frequencies for frequency conversion.

Local oscillator

Provides a stable reference used by mixers and synthesizers.

LNA

Low-noise amplifier boosts weak received signals while adding minimal noise.

PA

Power amplifier raises transmit RF power before the antenna.

Demodulator

Recovers the information from the modulated carrier.

RF Safety

Transmitters can produce hazardous RF exposure and high voltages. Antenna placement, power level, frequency, duty cycle, and distance all matter. Follow equipment instructions and applicable RF exposure requirements.
