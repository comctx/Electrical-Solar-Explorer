Transformer Types & Uses

Common transformer arrangements and what they are used for.

Step-DownSecondary voltage is lower than primary voltage. Common in power supplies and low-voltage equipment.Lower voltage
Step-UpSecondary voltage is higher than primary voltage. Used where a higher AC voltage is required.Higher voltage
Isolation TransformerOften near a 1:1 ratio and used for galvanic isolation between circuits.Isolation
Center-TappedA secondary winding with a midpoint connection, useful for split supplies and full-wave rectifier circuits.Split output
AutotransformerUses a shared winding rather than fully separate primary and secondary windings. It does not provide normal galvanic isolation.Shared winding
ToroidalRing-shaped core with compact size, good magnetic containment, and often low stray field.Compact
Current Transformer (CT)Produces a scaled current for metering and protection systems.Measurement
Audio / Signal TransformerUsed for impedance matching, isolation, and signal coupling in specialized circuits.Signals
Flyback TransformerStores and transfers energy in switched-mode circuits rather than behaving like a simple 50/60 Hz transformer.Switching supplies
Transformer circuits can involve hazardous voltages. A current-transformer secondary should not be left open-circuit while its primary conductor is energized; dangerous secondary voltage can result. Follow equipment and manufacturer instructions.
