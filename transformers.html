Beginner Start Here

New to transformers? These six ideas explain what is happening before you get into ratios, VA, impedance, and magnetic flux.

What is a transformer?

A transformer transfers AC electrical energy from one winding to another through a changing magnetic field. The two windings are usually called the primary and the secondary.

Primary vs. secondary

The primary is connected to the source. The secondary supplies the load. The number of turns on each winding determines the ideal voltage ratio.

What is magnetic coupling?

AC in the primary creates a changing magnetic field in the core. That changing field passes through the secondary winding and induces a voltage there.

Step-up vs. step-down

More secondary turns than primary turns raises voltage. Fewer secondary turns lowers voltage. For roughly the same VA, lower voltage allows proportionally higher current.

Why is AC important?

A transformer needs changing magnetic flux. Normal steady DC does not provide continuous transformer action and can overheat a winding if applied to a transformer not designed for it.

What does isolation mean?

With separate primary and secondary windings, energy crosses magnetically without a direct conductive connection between the two circuits. An autotransformer is different because it shares a winding.

In Other Words
Primary AC creates a changing magnetic field.
The changing field induces voltage in the secondary.
The turns ratio sets the ideal voltage and current relationship.

Primary voltage

The supply voltage the primary winding is designed to receive. Some units have multiple taps.

Secondary voltage

The rated output voltage, usually specified at a particular load and frequency.

VA rating

Volt-amperes describe the transformer's apparent-power capability. Do not treat VA as identical to watts for every AC load.

Frequency

Use at the frequency the transformer is designed for. A 60 Hz transformer should not automatically be assumed suitable at 50 Hz.

Current ratings

Rated current follows approximately from VA ÷ voltage, but the actual nameplate and winding configuration control.

Taps / wiring diagram

Multiple leads may represent taps, dual primaries, dual secondaries, series/parallel connections, or a center tap.

Core & Frequency Guide

Transformer core material and operating frequency are closely related.

50/60 Hz Power Transformers

  • Often use laminated electrical-steel cores.
  • Need many turns and a relatively large core.
  • Designed around mains-frequency magnetic flux.
  • Common in linear power supplies and utility-frequency equipment.

High-Frequency Transformers

  • Often use ferrite cores.
  • Can be much smaller for the same power level.
  • Used in switching power supplies, converters, and chargers.
  • Design must account for switching waveform, core loss, skin effect, and insulation.

Isolation Transformer vs. Autotransformer

They can both change voltage, but electrically they are very different.

Isolation Transformer

  • Separate primary and secondary windings.
  • Provides galvanic isolation between circuits when designed and used for that purpose.
  • Can be 1:1, step-up, or step-down.
  • Often used where separation between source and load is important.

Autotransformer

  • Primary and secondary share part of one winding.
  • Normally does not provide galvanic isolation.
  • Can be smaller and use less copper for some voltage-conversion ratios.
  • Common in voltage adjustment, buck/boost, and other specialized applications.
Never assume a transformer provides isolation just because it changes voltage. Check the actual winding arrangement, wiring diagram, and manufacturer documentation.
