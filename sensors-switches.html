Photoelectric Sensor Modes

ModeArrangementNotes
Through-beamSeparate emitter and receiver face each other.Long range and strong sensing margin; needs alignment and wiring at both sides.
RetroreflectiveEmitter/receiver are together and aim at a reflector.One powered sensor location; polarized versions help with shiny targets.
Diffuse reflectiveLight reflects directly from the object.Simple installation; range depends on target color, finish, size, and angle.
Background suppressionOptics evaluate returned-light geometry.Helps distinguish a target from a background at a different distance.

PNP, NPN, Sourcing & Sinking

PNP sensor output

Common industrial DC convention where an active output sources positive voltage to the load or PLC input.

NPN sensor output

Common industrial DC convention where an active output provides a path toward 0 V.

Sensor and PLC/input circuits must be electrically compatible. Manufacturer wiring diagrams take priority because pinouts, wire colors, and conventions can vary.

Important Sensor Specifications

SpecificationMeaning
Sensing distanceNominal distance at which the target can be detected under specified test conditions.
HysteresisDifference between the turn-on and turn-off positions; helps prevent chatter near the switching point.
Response time / frequencyHow quickly the sensor can recognize changing targets.
Output typeRelay, transistor, PNP, NPN, analog, or communication output depending on model.
Supply voltageVoltage range required to power the sensor.
Ingress ratingEnvironmental protection against dust and water where specified.
Light-on / dark-onDefines whether a photoelectric output is active when light is received or when the beam is blocked.

Where They Are Used

Conveyors

Detect boxes, count products, confirm position, and stop travel.

Machine tools

Confirm home/end positions and monitor guards or fixtures.

Packaging

Detect products, labels, containers, and registration marks.

Hydraulic / pneumatic cylinders

Magnetic sensors can confirm piston position without mechanical contact.

Doors & gates

Limit switches and magnetic sensors can indicate open/closed position.

Automation / PLCs

Sensors provide the input information a control system uses to make decisions.
