Common Switch Families

Pushbutton

Momentary or maintained operator control. START buttons often use normally-open contacts, while STOP controls commonly use normally-closed contacts in control systems.

Operator control
Toggle Switch

Lever-operated switch available in SPST, SPDT, DPDT, center-off, momentary, and maintained versions.

Manual control
Rocker Switch

Rocks between positions and is common on appliances, equipment panels, and power controls.

Panel switch
Slide Switch

A sliding actuator selects between positions. Common in low-voltage electronics and compact devices.

Compact
Rotary / Selector Switch

Turns to select one of several positions or circuits. Industrial selector switches may be two-position, three-position, spring-return, or maintained.

Multi-position
Key Switch

A selector or maintained switch that requires a key and can restrict who may operate or change a machine mode.

Restricted control
Emergency Stop

A purpose-designed emergency control, often a red mushroom operator with positive-opening contacts and manual reset. It belongs in a properly engineered safety circuit, not as an ordinary on/off switch.

Safety control
DIP Switch

A bank of miniature switches used to set addresses, modes, options, or configuration in electronic equipment.

Configuration
Reed Switch

Magnetically operated contacts sealed inside a glass capsule. Common in doors, level sensing, and position detection.

Magnetic

Pole & Throw: SPST, SPDT, DPST, DPDT

“Pole” tells how many separate circuits are switched. “Throw” tells how many output positions each pole can connect to.

SPST

Single Pole, Single Throw. One circuit, simple on/off action.

SPDT

Single Pole, Double Throw. One common terminal selects between two outputs.

DPST

Double Pole, Single Throw. Two separate circuits turn on or off together.

DPDT

Double Pole, Double Throw. Two circuits each switch between two paths. It can be used for many routing functions, including polarity reversal in suitable low-voltage circuits.

Process & Condition Switches

Pressure Switch

Changes contact state when pressure reaches an adjustable or fixed threshold. Used on compressors, pumps, hydraulics, and HVAC systems.

Float / Level Switch

Detects liquid level using a float, reed device, tilt mechanism, or other sensing method.

Flow Switch

Detects whether liquid or air flow is present or has reached a specified condition.

Temperature Switch / Thermostat

Changes state when temperature crosses a setpoint. Bimetal and electronic versions are common.

Vacuum Switch

Operates from negative pressure and is used in vacuum handling, pumps, and process equipment.

Tilt / Mercury-Free Tilt Switch

Changes state with orientation or motion. Modern designs often use a rolling ball or electronic sensor rather than mercury.

Switch Action & Ratings

TermMeaning
MomentaryReturns to its normal state when the operator releases it.
MaintainedStays in the selected state until moved again.
Snap actionInternal spring mechanism changes the contacts quickly once the actuator reaches its operating point.
Make-before-breakNew contact path closes before the old path opens.
Break-before-makeOld path opens before the new path closes.
Contact bounceMechanical contacts may rapidly open and close for a short time during switching. Digital circuits often need debounce filtering.
Voltage ratingMaximum circuit voltage the switch is designed to handle under stated conditions.
Current ratingMaximum current for the specified load type and voltage. AC, DC, resistive, lamp, and motor ratings may differ substantially.
Mechanical lifeExpected number of physical operating cycles without necessarily carrying rated electrical load.
Electrical lifeExpected switching life while making and breaking the specified electrical load.
A switch that is safe for one load is not automatically safe for another. Motor, lamp, capacitive, inductive, AC, and DC loads can stress contacts differently, so use the manufacturer’s actual switching ratings.

PNP, NPN, Sourcing & Sinking

PNP sensor output

Common industrial DC convention where an active output sources positive voltage to the load or PLC input.

NPN sensor output

Common industrial DC convention where an active output provides a path toward 0 V.

Sensor and PLC/input circuits must be electrically compatible. Manufacturer wiring diagrams take priority because pinouts, wire colors, and conventions can vary.

SpecificationMeaning
Sensing distanceNominal distance at which the target can be detected under specified test conditions.

Where They Are Used

Conveyors

Detect boxes, count products, confirm position, and stop travel.

Machine tools

Confirm home/end positions and monitor guards or fixtures.

Packaging

Detect products, labels, containers, and registration marks.

Hydraulic / pneumatic cylinders

Magnetic sensors can confirm piston position without mechanical contact.

Doors & gates

Limit switches and magnetic sensors can indicate open/closed position.

Automation / PLCs

Sensors provide the input information a control system uses to make decisions.
