Beginner Start Here

An electric motor converts electrical energy into mechanical rotation. The motor is only part of the system: starting, switching, protection, speed control, sensing, and the mechanical load all matter.

Stator

The stationary magnetic part of the motor. In many AC motors, windings in the stator create a rotating magnetic field.

Stationary
Rotor

The rotating part inside the stator. Electromagnetic forces produce torque on the rotor.

Rotating
Shaft

Carries torque from the rotor to the machine, fan, pump, gearbox, pulley, or other load.

Mechanical output
Bearings

Support the shaft and let it rotate with low friction. Bearing wear is a common source of motor noise and vibration.

Support
Windings

Insulated coils of wire that create magnetic fields when current flows.

Electromagnetic
Cooling fan

Moves air over the motor frame or through the motor to remove heat.

Temperature control

Motor Types

Single-Phase Induction

Common in homes and small equipment. Many use a start winding and capacitor to create starting torque.

Three-Phase Induction

Widely used in industry. Three-phase power naturally creates a rotating magnetic field.

DC Brushed Motor

Uses brushes and a commutator to switch current in the rotating armature.

Brushless DC (BLDC)

Uses electronic commutation instead of brushes. Common in fans, tools, vehicles, and automation.

Synchronous Motor

Rotor speed locks to the rotating magnetic field rather than operating with induction-motor slip.

Stepper / Servo

Used when position and motion control are important. Servos normally include feedback; steppers move in discrete steps.

Associated Motor Parts & Controls

  • Start capacitor: provides phase shift and extra starting torque for certain single-phase motors.
  • Run capacitor: remains in the circuit while running on capacitor-run designs to improve operating characteristics.
  • Centrifugal switch: mechanically disconnects a start winding or start capacitor after the motor accelerates on some designs.
  • Potential/current relay: can switch start components based on motor electrical conditions.
  • Contactor: electrically operated power switch used to connect and disconnect a motor.
  • Overload relay: protects against sustained motor overcurrent/overheating; it is different from short-circuit protection.
  • Motor starter: commonly combines a contactor with overload protection and control functions.
  • Fuse/circuit breaker: provides branch-circuit and short-circuit protection as designed for the system.
  • VFD: variable-frequency drive changes AC frequency and voltage to control compatible AC motor speed and torque.
  • Soft starter: reduces electrical and mechanical stress during starting without providing full variable-speed control like a VFD.
  • Encoder: measures shaft position, speed, or direction for feedback systems.
  • Thermal protector: opens or signals when motor temperature exceeds a safe limit.
  • Brushes: sliding electrical contacts used in brushed motors.
  • Commutator: segmented rotating switch used with brushes in DC motors and universal motors.
  • Coupling: connects the motor shaft to another shaft while accommodating the intended alignment/motion.
  • Gearbox: trades speed for torque or changes the mechanical relationship between motor and load.
