Beginner Start Here

LEDs and LCDs both display information, but they work in completely different ways.

LED

A light-emitting diode produces light when current flows through its semiconductor junction in the correct direction.

Makes light
LCD

A liquid-crystal display controls how light passes through polarized layers. The liquid crystal itself does not create light.

Controls light
LED polarity

The anode is the positive side and the cathode is the negative side. A normal LED lights only when forward biased.

Directional device
LCD backlight

Many LCD panels use LEDs as a backlight. Reflective LCDs can instead use ambient light.

Separate light source
Easy way to remember it: an LED emits light; an LCD modulates light.

How an LCD Works

Liquid-crystal layer

An electric field changes the orientation of liquid-crystal molecules. Polarizers and that molecular orientation control how much light reaches your eye.

Segments or pixels

Simple LCDs use shaped segments. Graphic LCDs use rows and columns of pixels. TFT LCDs add a transistor at each pixel for improved control.

Backlight

The backlight is a separate light source—often LEDs—behind or along the edge of the panel.

AC drive

Raw passive LCD glass is normally driven with alternating waveforms. A long-term DC bias can damage or degrade the display.
