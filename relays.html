Beginner Start Here

A relay uses an electrical control signal to open or close contacts. A contactor is the same general idea, built for heavier-duty switching.

What is a relay?

An electromechanical relay energizes a coil, creates a magnetic field, and moves an armature that changes one or more contacts.

What do COM, NO, and NC mean?

COM is the moving common contact. NO means normally open. NC means normally closed. “Normally” means the coil is not energized.

What is a contactor?

A contactor is a heavy-duty relay intended for switching loads such as motors, heaters, lighting, and industrial equipment.

Coil and contacts are different circuits

The coil is the control side. The contacts switch the load side. In an electromechanical relay they are usually electrically isolated from each other.

Why add a flyback diode?

A DC relay coil stores magnetic energy. When current is interrupted, the collapsing field can create a high-voltage spike. A flyback diode provides a safe current path and protects the driver.

Contact ratings matter

AC and DC ratings can differ, and motors, solenoids, lamps, and other inductive or high-inrush loads can be harder on contacts than a simple resistive load.

In Other Words
Coil OFF: COM is connected to NC.
Coil ON: COM moves to NO.
A contactor is a relay designed for larger switching duties.

Relay vs. Contactor

Relay

  • Common for control signals and smaller loads.
  • Available with many contact arrangements such as SPDT and DPDT.
  • Can provide galvanic isolation between coil and contacts.
  • Mechanical, reed, latching, time-delay, and solid-state versions exist.

Contactor

  • Designed for repeated switching of larger loads.
  • Common in motor, heater, lighting, and industrial control.
  • Often includes auxiliary contacts for seal-in, interlocking, or status.
  • Frequently used with overload relays and other motor-protection devices.
