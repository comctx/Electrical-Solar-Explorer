Solid-State Relay (SSR)

An electronically controlled switch with no moving power contacts. It often uses an optocoupler plus a TRIAC, SCR pair, MOSFETs, or other semiconductor output stage.

No moving contacts
SCR / Thyristor

A latching semiconductor switch. Once triggered and carrying enough current, it remains on until current falls below its holding level.

High-power switch
TRIAC

A bidirectional AC switching device often used for mains control, heaters, lamps, and some motor loads.

AC control
DIAC

A two-terminal trigger device often used to fire TRIACs in phase-control circuits.

Trigger device
Optocoupler

Transfers a control signal using light, providing electrical isolation between input and output circuits when properly designed.

Isolation
Zero-Cross Switching

Turns an AC solid-state switch on near the AC voltage zero crossing to reduce certain switching transients and EMI.

AC SSR vs. DC SSR

AC-output SSR

Commonly uses TRIACs or back-to-back SCRs. Many are designed to turn off naturally as AC current passes through zero.

DC-output SSR

Commonly uses MOSFETs or transistor stages. DC does not naturally cross zero every half-cycle, so the output device must actively interrupt current.

AC and DC SSRs are not interchangeable just because their current ratings look similar. Output topology, voltage ratings, load type, and turn-off behavior are different.

SCR & TRIAC Concepts

SCR latching

A gate pulse starts conduction, but the gate does not normally turn an SCR off. Current must fall below the holding current or be commutated off.

TRIAC bidirectional conduction

A TRIAC can conduct in both directions, making it convenient for many AC control applications.

Phase-angle control

Delaying turn-on within each AC half-cycle changes the average power delivered to a compatible load.

Zero-cross control

Switching whole AC cycles near zero crossing is common for heater control and other loads where fast phase control is unnecessary.

Where Solid-State Switching Is Used

Industrial heaters

SSRs and SCR controllers provide fast, silent cycling without mechanical contact wear.

Motor controls

Soft starters commonly use SCR pairs to ramp motor voltage during starting.

Lighting control

TRIAC phase control is common with compatible AC lighting loads.

Power supplies

MOSFETs and other switches operate at high frequency in switching converters.

Automation outputs

PLC output modules may use transistor, TRIAC, or relay outputs depending on the application.

Inverters and drives

High-power solid-state switches synthesize controlled AC from a DC bus.

Solid-State Safety

“Off” does not always mean electrically isolated. SSRs can leak current, semiconductor devices can fail shorted, and many power circuits remain hazardous even after a control signal is removed. Use proper disconnecting means, verify de-energization, discharge stored energy, and follow the equipment manufacturer’s service procedures.
