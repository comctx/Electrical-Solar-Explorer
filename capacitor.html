Beginner Start Here

New to capacitors? These six ideas explain what they do before you get into timing, reactance, ESR, and filters.

What is a capacitor?

A capacitor is a component that stores electrical energy by separating charge between two conductive surfaces. It can charge up, hold energy for a time, and then release it.

What does capacitance mean?

Capacitance tells you how much electric charge a capacitor can store for a given voltage. Its unit is the farad, written F. Common values are µF, nF, and pF.

Why does voltage change gradually?

A capacitor does not change its voltage instantly. As current flows in or out, the voltage rises or falls over time. Resistance and capacitance together determine how fast that happens.

What happens when it is fully charged?

In a simple DC circuit, current falls toward zero as the capacitor approaches the supply voltage. If the circuit changes, the stored charge can flow back out.

Why do polarity and voltage rating matter?

Many electrolytic and tantalum capacitors are polarized and must be connected the correct way. Every capacitor also has a maximum voltage rating that should not be exceeded.

Where are capacitors used?

You find them in power supplies, timing circuits, filters, motor circuits, audio equipment, radios, energy storage, noise suppression, and electronic control circuits.

Capacitor Types & Uses

A quick guide to common capacitor families, polarity, and where they are usually found.

Ceramic Small, non-polarized, inexpensive, and common in high-frequency bypassing, decoupling, timing, and RF circuits. Non-polarized
Aluminum Electrolytic High capacitance for power-supply filtering and energy storage. Usually polarized and commonly used at lower frequencies. Usually polarized
Tantalum Compact polarized capacitors with stable capacitance. Often used in electronics where space matters. Polarized
Film Non-polarized, stable, and often used in filters, audio, timing, snubbers, and AC applications. Non-polarized
Supercapacitor Very high capacitance for short-term energy storage, backup power, memory hold-up, and burst-energy applications. Often polarized
Motor Start / Run Special AC capacitors used with single-phase motors. Start capacitors are intermittent-duty; run capacitors are continuous-duty. Application-specific
