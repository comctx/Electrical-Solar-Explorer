Beginner Start Here

A circuit breaker is an automatically operated switch that opens a circuit when current or fault conditions exceed what the breaker is designed to allow.

MCB — Miniature Circuit Breaker

Compact low-voltage breaker commonly used on smaller branch circuits. Many use thermal-magnetic trip elements.

Small branch circuits
MCCB — Molded-Case Circuit Breaker

Larger low-voltage breaker family used for feeders, machinery, and higher-current circuits. Many models have adjustable or electronic trip functions.

Feeders / larger loads
Air Circuit Breaker

Uses air as the interrupting medium. Low-voltage power circuit breakers often use arc chutes to cool and divide the arc.

Low-voltage distribution
Vacuum Circuit Breaker

Interrupts current inside a sealed vacuum interrupter. Vacuum breakers are widely used in medium-voltage equipment.

Medium voltage
SF₆ Circuit Breaker

Uses sulfur hexafluoride gas or related gas-insulated technology to help interrupt arcs in medium- and high-voltage equipment.

Medium / high voltage
Oil Circuit Breaker

Uses insulating oil to help cool and extinguish the arc. It is mainly associated with older high-voltage switchgear and legacy systems.

Legacy high-voltage systems
In Other Words
MCB and MCCB describe breaker construction and application classes.
Air, vacuum, SF₆, and oil describe how the breaker controls the arc when opening.
A breaker must interrupt the available fault current safely — not just carry normal load current.

Protection Devices

Fuse

Melts and opens when current exceeds its time-current capability.

Circuit Breaker

Automatically opens on specified overcurrent or fault conditions and can normally be reset after the cause is corrected.

GFCI / RCD

Detects imbalance between conductors that can indicate leakage to ground or earth.

AFCI

Detects signatures associated with certain hazardous arcing conditions.

Surge Protective Device

Limits transient overvoltage by diverting surge energy.

Thermal Overload

Protects motors from sustained overload heating; it is not a substitute for short-circuit protection.

Arc Interruption Advanced

Air / Arc Chute

The opening arc is stretched, cooled, and divided into smaller arc segments by arc runners and splitter plates until current can be interrupted.

Vacuum

Contacts separate inside a sealed vacuum bottle. The arc is supported by metal vapor briefly, then dielectric strength recovers rapidly after current interruption.

SF₆ Gas

Gas flow and dielectric properties are used to cool and deionize the arc path in suitable interrupter designs.

Oil

Arc energy decomposes part of the oil and creates gases that help cool and deionize the arc zone. Oil condition and fire risk are important maintenance concerns.

Interrupting Rating, Coordination & Maintenance Advanced

Interrupting Rating

A breaker must be able to safely interrupt the prospective fault current at its rated system voltage. Normal ampere rating alone does not tell you this.

Available Fault Current

Depends on the source, transformer impedance, conductor impedance, generators, motors, and other system conditions.

Selective Coordination

Protection is coordinated so the device closest to a fault clears first when the system design and rules require it, minimizing unnecessary outages.

Current Limiting

Some breakers and fuses can substantially reduce peak let-through current and energy compared with the available prospective fault.

Inspection & Testing

Larger breakers may require inspection, cleaning, lubrication, contact checks, insulation testing, trip-unit testing, and mechanism maintenance according to manufacturer instructions.

After a Major Fault

A breaker that interrupted a severe fault should not simply be reset without determining the fault cause and checking the breaker and connected equipment as required by the manufacturer.

SF₆ is a very potent greenhouse gas. Equipment containing it requires appropriate leak prevention, recovery, servicing, and end-of-life handling. Modern switchgear increasingly uses alternative gases, vacuum interruption, or other designs depending on voltage class and manufacturer.

Surge & Transient Parts

MOV

Voltage-dependent device commonly used to clamp transient overvoltage.

TVS Diode

Fast semiconductor transient suppressor for lower-voltage electronics.

Gas Discharge Tube

Handles large surge energy by ionizing a gas path at high voltage.

Protection Is Layered

Different protective devices solve different hazards. Overcurrent protection, ground-fault protection, arc-fault protection, surge protection, motor overload protection, and disconnecting means are not interchangeable.
